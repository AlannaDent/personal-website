# Visual style reference

Style direction for the bookstore game, captured 16 September 2026 from three reference
paintings of bookshop storefronts.

## The look in one line
Loose watercolor illustration: soft, layered washes with visible pen-and-ink outlines,
warm and saturated colors, and greenery spilling over everything.

## What the references share
- **Medium:** watercolor with fine ink linework. Paper texture shows through. Edges are
  slightly imperfect, never crisp vector shapes.
- **Color:** rich but soft. Brick red, slate blue, deep forest green, dusty pink, warm
  golden window light. Shadows are colored (blues and purples), not grey.
- **Greenery everywhere:** climbing vines up the walls, window boxes overflowing with
  flowers, ivy over the awning, potted plants by the door.
- **Warm interiors glowing out:** windows show packed bookshelves lit from inside, drawing
  the eye in. The shop always looks open and inviting.
- **Whimsical clutter:** a cat in the window, a chalkboard sign, a hanging lantern, a
  book cart on the pavement, a sparrow on the sign. Small details reward looking closely.
- **Single-building portraits:** each shop is shown straight-on, centered, filling the
  frame like a portrait. Good model for how each game stage could be presented.

## What to avoid
- "Beige mom" aesthetic: no washed-out neutrals, no grey-on-cream minimalism, no sparse
  empty walls. Color and clutter are the point.
- Hard-edged flat vector art or pixel art. Everything should feel hand-painted.
- Cold or clinical lighting.

## People
Same watercolor-and-ink hand as the buildings, but characters are simple and readable:
- Round, friendly proportions with small features and rosy cheeks. Faces are a few strokes.
- Personality comes from outfits and props, not faces: striped jumper, red scarf, beret,
  wicker basket, walking cane, tote bag of books. Every customer should be recognisable by
  what they carry and wear.
- Muted-but-rich clothing colors (navy, brick red, mustard, plaid) that sit comfortably
  against the building palette.
- Variety in age and shape. Grandmothers on benches are as welcome as tourists with totes.

## Real-world anchors (Chatham and Cape Cod)
Things to wink at, painted in the style above, never photorealistic:
- **The real Chatham bookshops:** dark-red trim and gold lettering on one, a striped
  awning, flower boxes and a hanging sign on the other. Both are narrow, glass-fronted,
  and stuffed with books to the ceiling. Good models for the storefront stage.
- **Little Free Libraries:** the stage-one shop. Real ones are tiny cabinets on posts or
  spindly legs, with a glass door, a small pitched roof, and a personality of their own:
  painted leaves, a succulent garden growing on the roof, stickers on the glass. Around
  twenty books fit, which is the joke.
- **Lighthouses:** white tower, black lantern room, attached grey-shingled keeper's house
  with red roof, white picket fence, on grass above the sea. Sunset skies in purple and
  peach. A holiday wreath on the tower is a real Cape tradition worth stealing. This is
  the model for the lighthouse specialty building.
- **Cape textures generally:** weathered grey shingles, white trim, picket fences, hydrangeas.

## Folder layout
```
reference/
  STYLE.md                       this file
  Building Style References/     paintings of bookshops: how it should look
  Building Real Inspiration/     photos of real places: what to wink at
  Person Style References/       illustrated people: how customers should look
```

The images are other artists' work or photos found online and are for private
inspiration only. They must never be used as game assets or published on the site.
All image files under `reference/` are excluded from git (see `.gitignore` in the
project root) so they cannot accidentally end up in a public repo.
